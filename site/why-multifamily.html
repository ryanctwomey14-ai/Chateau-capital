The Case

Four structural advantages

Not opinions about the market, features of how the asset itself works.

01

Vacancy is diversified inside the asset

A single-family rental has one tenant. When they leave, your income is zero and your mortgage is not. A 200-unit community with a 6% vacancy rate is still collecting 94% of its rent, and it does so every month, through every economic climate.

This is the most under-appreciated advantage of scale, and it is the reason institutional capital concentrates here.

02

The tax treatment is genuinely different

The tax code treats buildings as though they wear out, allowing owners to deduct a portion of the building's value each year as depreciation, even while the property is appreciating. A cost segregation study accelerates those deductions into the early years.

The practical effect for a limited partner: a meaningful portion of the cash you receive may be offset by paper losses reported on your K-1, and taxed later at sale rather than now at your marginal rate.

Tax outcomes depend entirely on your personal circumstances. Nothing here is tax advice, take your K-1 to your own CPA.

03

Rents reprice; your mortgage doesn't

Apartment leases typically run twelve months. When inflation raises the cost of everything, rents can follow within a year, while a fixed-rate mortgage stays exactly where it was, repaid in dollars that are worth progressively less.

Few assets combine a repricing revenue line with a fixed liability. It is why apartments have historically been treated as an inflation-responsive holding.

04

Value can be manufactured

Because apartments are valued as a multiple of net operating income, adding $100,000 of annual NOI at a 5.5% capitalisation rate adds roughly $1.8m of value, whether that comes from renovated units, better expense control, or recovering utility costs.

You are not waiting for a market to reward you. You are running a business that either performs or doesn't.

None of that matters if the operator is careless, which is the next thing worth checking.

Taxation

The reason your CPA keeps mentioning real estate

For a high-income professional, the tax treatment is often worth more than the headline return.

Here are the four mechanisms that do the work. Each has conditions and limits, and none of them apply universally, which is exactly why this section belongs in a conversation with your own adviser rather than in a brochure.

Discuss your situation

Depreciation

Residential buildings are depreciated over 27.5 years. That annual deduction flows through to you on a K-1 and can offset the distributions you receive.

Cost segregation

An engineering study reclassifies components, appliances, flooring, site work, into shorter depreciation lives, accelerating deductions into the early years of the hold.

Capital gains treatment

Profit on sale is generally taxed at long-term capital gains rates rather than ordinary income rates, subject to depreciation recapture.

Step-up in basis

Under current law, heirs may receive a stepped-up basis on inherited interests, a significant consideration in estate planning.

Chateau Capital does not provide tax advice. Tax treatment depends on your individual circumstances and on legislation that changes. Consult a qualified tax adviser.

The Thesis

Why we believe this part of the cycle rewards patient buyers

Between 2021 and 2023, developers delivered an unusually large volume of new apartments, and interest rates rose faster than in any period in four decades. Together those two facts pushed values down and forced a number of owners to sell assets they had intended to hold.

What that produces

  • A reset in basis. Assets are trading meaningfully below their 2021 peaks and, in a number of cases, below the cost of building the same property today. Replacement cost is a floor that tends to reassert itself.
  • A collapsing supply pipeline. New construction starts have fallen sharply because the numbers no longer work for developers. Apartments take two to three years to deliver, so a slowdown in starts today is a shortage of new supply in the years a current acquisition would be held.
  • Structural demand. Elevated mortgage rates and home prices have made owning materially more expensive than renting in most major markets. Every household that postpones a home purchase remains a renter.
  • Less competition for deals. Sponsors who over-levered in 2021 are not bidding. That is a meaningful advantage to buyers with capital and patience.

And what would prove us wrong

If rates rise substantially further from here, if employment deteriorates badly enough to reduce household formation, or if insurance and tax costs keep escalating faster than rents, this thesis weakens. We underwrite assuming some of that happens, see our risk management approach, but no underwriting survives every scenario.

Risk

What can go wrong

You will not find this section on most sponsor websites. You should ask why.

These are speculative, leveraged investments in a single asset class. If a property cannot service its debt and cannot be refinanced or sold, limited partner equity can be reduced to zero. This is not a remote scenario, it happened to real investors in real deals between 2022 and 2024.

There is no public market for these interests and no redemption right. Transfers generally require sponsor consent. If your circumstances change, you cannot simply sell.

Distributions are targets based on projections, not obligations. If occupancy falls, expenses rise, or a lender requires cash to be retained, distributions can be cut or paused without notice.

Some structures permit the sponsor to request additional capital. Declining to participate can dilute your position. Whether a capital call is possible, and on what terms, is disclosed in each offering's documents, read that section carefully in any deal, ours included.

Loans mature. If rates at maturity are higher than underwritten, refinancing may require additional equity or a sale at an unfavourable time. Rate caps expire and can be expensive to replace.

You are delegating every decision to a sponsor. Poor underwriting, poor execution, or poor governance will cost you money regardless of how good the market is. Diligence the operator at least as thoroughly as the property.

Property insurance premiums and municipal tax assessments have risen sharply in several markets. These are among the fastest-moving expense lines in the business and can compress returns even when occupancy and rents perform.
