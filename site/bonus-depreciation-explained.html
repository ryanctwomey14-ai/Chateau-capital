How 100% Bonus Depreciation Works, and Why It Exists

A first-year deduction on qualifying components, and the policy reasoning behind letting investors take it.

David Snider / 24 August 2025 / 5 min read

Bonus depreciation allows real estate investors to deduct the cost of qualifying assets in the same tax year they are placed in service, rather than spreading the deduction across several years.

What qualifies

In an apartment community, qualifying assets commonly include appliances, furniture, fixtures, HVAC systems, lighting and landscaping. Qualified Improvement Property, meaning certain interior improvements to non-residential portions of a building, can also qualify.

The structure itself does not. Land never does.

Cost segregation is what makes it usable

Investors reach these deductions through a cost segregation study: an engineering analysis that identifies and reclassifies building components onto shorter recovery schedules, typically five, seven or fifteen years, instead of the 27.5-year schedule that applies to the building.

Once reclassified, those components can be eligible for the first-year deduction. The total deduction across the life of the asset does not change. Its timing does, and timing is worth money.

Why the provision exists

Bonus depreciation was written to accelerate economic activity by encouraging private investment in income-producing assets, including housing. The stated policy aims are consistent across the legislative record.

  • Stimulate private spending. Allowing an immediate deduction motivates owners to buy, build and improve sooner rather than later.
  • Accelerate job creation. Construction and renovation work supports trades, engineering, property management and the local services around them.
  • Expand housing supply. Multifamily adds units without expanding into undeveloped land, which matters given the shortage of affordable housing.
  • Use private capital instead of public spending. Rather than funding housing programmes directly, the incentive mobilises private capital toward the same end.

What it does downstream

When an investor uses the deduction, the capital freed up tends to be reinvested. The second-order effects are the reason the provision survives politically:

  • Renovation keeps older communities competitive, safe and livable, at a fraction of the cost of new construction
  • Each project engages electricians, plumbers, HVAC technicians, roofers, landscapers and managers
  • Upgrades often include energy-efficient appliances, better insulation and water-saving fixtures
  • Improved properties generate higher property tax revenue, funding schools and services without raising rates
  • Renovating existing stock avoids the higher rents that new development requires

Confirm the current rate before you model anything

The bonus depreciation percentage is set by legislation and has been changed and restored more than once in recent years. Do not rely on a percentage quoted in any article, including this one. Confirm the rate in force for the year the asset is placed in service with your CPA.
