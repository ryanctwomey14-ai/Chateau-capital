What Is a Real Estate Syndication, and Why Is It Built for Passive Investors?

Own a share of a large apartment community without being the landlord. Here is how the structure actually works.

David Snider / 9 July 2025 / 6 min read

Ever wish you could own a large apartment community without being the landlord? That is what a real estate syndication is for.

If you have never come across one before, you are not alone. Once you understand the structure, it becomes clear why it is a long-standing strategy for people with capital but no spare time.

What a syndication is

A syndication is a group investment: a legal structure in which multiple investors pool capital to purchase a large, income-producing property. There are two sides to it.

General partners, also called sponsors, find, underwrite and acquire the deal, arrange the financing and renovations, and manage the business plan and day-to-day operations.

Limited partners provide the capital, own a proportional share of the asset, receive cash flow, tax benefits and a share of equity at sale, and carry no management responsibility whatsoever.

A worked example

Suppose Chateau Capital identifies a 120-unit apartment community in a growing Southeast market:

  • Purchase price: $12M
  • Renovation and reserves: $2M
  • Total project: $14M
  • Equity raised from investors: $4M
  • Mortgage financing: $10M

You invest $100,000 as a limited partner. In return you receive quarterly cash distributions, a K-1 reporting your share of income and depreciation, ownership in a real asset, and a share of profits at refinance or sale.

You do not fix toilets. You do not deal with tenants. You own the asset passively while operators run it.

When I came across syndications, I realised I could go bigger and faster while protecting my time and my income.

I had already renovated homes and built a rental portfolio, and I loved real estate. What I did not want was another full-time job.

Our investors at Chateau Capital are physicians, business owners and professionals. People with capital, but not time. This structure was built for them.

In summary

  • A syndication is a group investment in a single large property
  • You invest passively, with no landlord duties
  • You earn cash flow, tax benefits and a share of appreciation
  • You receive a K-1 each year rather than a 1099
  • Your capital is committed for the life of the deal, typically five to seven years
