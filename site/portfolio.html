Case Study

Ashford Commons

288 units, Fort Worth. Acquired 2019, sold 2024.

A well-located 1998-vintage community with original interiors, below-market rents, and an expense line nobody had examined in a decade. The seller had owned it for sixteen years and managed it themselves.

What we did

  • Renovated 211 of 288 units on natural turnover, never by displacing a paying resident
  • Re-tendered the landscaping, waste and turn contracts
  • Appealed the tax assessment in two consecutive years
  • Introduced utility billback where the leases permitted it
  • Replaced on-site management in month four

When it got hard

Between 2022 and 2024 a great many multifamily sponsors got into serious trouble. You deserve to know where we stood, and we would rather tell you than have you find out later.

What went wrong across the industry

The sponsors who failed generally shared three habits. They bought at historically low capitalisation rates and assumed those rates would hold. They financed long-term assets with short-term floating-rate debt and thin interest rate caps. And they underwrote rent growth that had no precedent outside 2021.

When rates rose, the caps expired, the debt service doubled, and refinancing was not available at any price that preserved investor equity. That is the mechanism behind almost every capital call and forced sale you read about.

Where we stood

We are not immune to a market, and we have made mistakes. What we did not do was finance long-term assets with short-term floating debt without a cap in place at close. Every asset in the portfolio entered the period with debt whose maturity extended beyond our planned hold.

Two of our business plans have taken longer than projected. On one asset we reduced the distribution for three quarters in order to fund a roof and HVAC programme that diligence had identified but that we chose to accelerate. We reported both in the quarter they happened, with the numbers and the plan, rather than in the quarter they became unavoidable.

Our commitment on bad news

If a property underperforms, you will hear it from us first, in writing, with the numbers and the plan. We would rather lose an investor to honesty than keep one through omission.
