Bonus Depreciation and Charitable Deductions: A Comparison

Two tools Congress created to let taxpayers reduce taxable income faster. They rhyme more than most people expect.

David Snider / 24 August 2025 / 5 min read

Bonus depreciation and the charitable contribution deduction are both instruments Congress created to let taxpayers reduce taxable income more quickly than they otherwise could. Comparing them side by side clarifies what each can and cannot do.

What they share

  • Both reduce taxable income, not tax owed. Neither creates a refundable credit.
  • Both have strict eligibility tests. Bonus depreciation applies only to qualified property with a recovery period of twenty years or less, purchased from an unrelated party and placed in service. Charitable deductions apply only to gifts to qualified organisations.
  • Both are capped. Bonus depreciation is limited to a legislated percentage of cost. Charitable deductions are limited by a percentage of adjusted gross income.
  • Both demand records. Receipts, purchase agreements and acknowledgment letters are required to substantiate either.

Limits on bonus depreciation

  • Property type. Only tangible property, and some software, with a recovery period of twenty years or less. Land, buildings and structural components generally do not qualify.
  • Acquisition. The asset must be purchased, not inherited or gifted, and from an unrelated party. Listed property such as vehicles must be used more than 50% for business.
  • Timing. The asset must be placed in service during the tax year. The deduction is optional and a taxpayer may elect out and depreciate normally.

Limits on charitable deductions

  • Income-based caps. Cash donations are generally limited to 60% of adjusted gross income. Non-cash gifts and gifts to certain private foundations face lower limits.
  • Qualified organisations only. Gifts to individuals, foreign charities and political organisations are not deductible.
  • Itemising required. Taxpayers taking the standard deduction get no benefit from charitable gifts.
  • No quid pro quo. Any benefit received in return reduces the deductible amount.

One meaningful difference

Unused charitable deductions can generally be carried forward. Unused bonus depreciation is effectively lost, although a taxpayer can elect out and spread the deduction over the normal schedule instead.

Understanding both helps in deciding how to structure business purchases and charitable giving in the same year.

Percentages change

Both the bonus depreciation rate and the AGI limits on charitable giving have been amended repeatedly. Verify the figures in force for the relevant tax year with your CPA rather than relying on any published article.
