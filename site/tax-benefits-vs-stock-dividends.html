Tax Benefits of Multifamily Investing vs. Stock Dividends

For a high earner, the choice between stocks and real estate often comes down to one thing: how the income is treated.

David Snider / 9 July 2025 / 4 min read

For high-income earners, the choice between equities and real estate often comes down to a single advantage: tax. Multifamily offers treatment that dividend-paying stocks structurally cannot match.

How stock dividends are treated

When you invest in dividend-paying stocks, the income you receive is taxed, either at your ordinary rate or at qualified dividend rates. Those returns can accumulate well, but they are not tax-advantaged, and your gains are exposed each year they arise.

Where real estate differs

  • Depreciation. Owners deduct a portion of the building's value each year, reducing taxable income even while cash flow remains strong.
  • Cost segregation and bonus depreciation. An engineering study reclassifies shorter-lived components onto faster schedules, concentrating deductions into the early years.
  • Capital gains treatment on exit. Profit on sale is generally taxed at long-term capital gains rates rather than ordinary rates, subject to depreciation recapture.
  • 1031 exchanges. In the right structure, gain can be deferred into a replacement property rather than realised.

Cash flow and shelter together

For a busy professional, the ability to earn a return and defer or reduce the tax on it is the difference that matters. Multifamily does not simply produce income. It changes the character of that income.

One qualification worth stating

These advantages are real but they are not automatic. Passive activity loss rules restrict how depreciation can be used, recapture applies on sale, and everything here depends on your individual circumstances. Take any offering's projections to your own CPA before relying on them.
