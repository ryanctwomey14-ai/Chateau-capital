Superior Returns on Capital: What the Long-Run Data Shows

Multifamily has been recognised by CBRE and NCREIF as a strong risk-adjusted performer over multi-decade periods. Here is why.

David Snider / 9 July 2025 / 4 min read

Multifamily has been identified by CBRE and other industry researchers as a strategy capable of producing strong capital returns relative to traditional equities. The advantage comes from a combination of factors rather than any single one.

What the research reports

CBRE's 2017 analysis reported average annual returns for multifamily of approximately 9.75% over the twenty-five year period from 1992 to 2017, exceeding the S&P 500 across that same window.

The National Council of Real Estate Investment Fiduciaries has likewise reported that multifamily assets have delivered strong risk-adjusted returns across multiple decades.

These are third-party historical figures covering specific periods, cited here as published. They are not Chateau Capital results, they are not a projection, and past performance does not indicate future results.

Four contributing factors

  • Stable and growing income. Rental income is consistent and can rise with inflation and demand, providing a buffer against volatility.
  • Capital appreciation. Value grows through property improvements and effective management, not only through market movement.
  • Tax treatment. Depreciation and cost segregation defer liability and improve after-tax outcomes.
  • Resilience in downturns. Historically, multifamily has tended to hold occupancy and rental income better than several other asset classes during recessions.

The honest conclusion

The combination of income, appreciation, tax treatment and resilience is what makes the asset class compelling. It does not make it riskless. Every one of those factors depends on the operator executing, which is the risk equities do not carry and this one does.
