How we invest

The criteria, the process, the discipline and the economics, in enough detail that you can hold us to them.

Philosophy

Four rules we don't break

Most losses in this business trace back to a rule someone decided to bend once.

Rule One

The deal must work on today's rents

If an acquisition requires above-trend rent growth to hit its numbers, it is not an investment, it is a forecast. We underwrite from in-place income and treat growth as upside, not as the plan.

Rule Two

Debt is where sponsors die

Fixed-rate or fully capped debt, matched to the business plan, with maturity well beyond our expected hold. We would rather accept a lower projected return than accept a refinancing deadline we cannot control.

Rule Three

We operate what we own

Asset management is not oversight from a distance. Weekly reviews with on-site teams against a fixed budget, and a renovation programme we specify, price and inspect ourselves.

Rule Four

Bad news travels first

Problems get reported in the quarter they occur, with the numbers and the remediation plan. An investor who learns about a problem late has been given a reason to distrust everything else.

Process

We review a hundred properties to buy one.

The value we add is mostly in what we decline.

01

Screen, around 100 opportunities

Everything that reaches us is screened against the buy box in minutes. Wrong market, wrong vintage, wrong size, wrong debt assumption: pass. This stage exists to protect the team's attention for the deals that deserve it.

02

Underwrite, about 15 opportunities

Full financial model built from the trailing twelve months of actual operating statements and the current rent roll, never from a broker's proforma. We rebuild the expense line from scratch, using our own operating history in that market rather than the seller's.

Every deal is then run through three cases: our base case, a downside case, and a stress case in which rents are flat, vacancy rises, and the exit capitalisation rate is materially higher than the entry rate. If the stress case loses investor capital, we stop.

03

Diligence, about 4 opportunities

Unit-by-unit interior inspection, third-party property condition and environmental reports, a full lease-file audit, an independent insurance quote before we rely on a number, and a market study of the forward supply pipeline. Deals die here regularly, and that is the point.

04

Acquire & execute, 1 acquisition

We close with committed equity and locked debt terms. Renovations begin on a defined schedule, unit by unit, at a specified cost per unit and a target rent premium we have already tested in that submarket. On-site staffing is reviewed within the first thirty days.

05

Operate, report, exit

Weekly performance reviews against budget. Quarterly written reports to limited partners with the property-level numbers. We sell when the market rewards selling, not because a fund clock says we must.

Execution

Where the return actually comes from

Four unglamorous levers. None of them require the market to cooperate.

Lever 01

Interior renovation

Flooring, fixtures, counters, appliances and paint on unit turnover, never by displacing a paying resident. Renovation spend is committed only where a tested rent premium supports it.

Lever 02

Expense discipline

Renegotiated service contracts, portfolio-scale insurance procurement, tax assessment appeals, and water and energy conservation. Every dollar removed from expenses adds roughly eighteen to value.

Lever 03

Income recapture

Utility billback where permitted, appropriate ancillary fees, parking and storage. Most acquired properties are leaving legitimate income uncollected because nobody was measuring it.

Lever 04

Resident retention

Turnover is the single largest controllable cost in an apartment community. Better maintenance response times and a community worth staying in beat rent increases that trigger a move-out.

Risk Management

How we try not to lose your money

Return is what gets discussed. Risk is what gets decided.

We stress-test before we bid

Flat rent growth, elevated vacancy, higher exit capitalisation rate than entry, and escalating insurance costs, modelled simultaneously, not one at a time. The stress case is what determines our maximum price.

Reserves are funded at closing, not hoped for

Operating and capital reserves are capitalised into the raise. A deal that needs every dollar of its equity to close has no margin for the first surprise.

Debt maturity beyond the hold

The most damaging outcome in this business is being forced to sell or refinance at the wrong moment. We buy time with loan structure, and we pay for it in projected return.

Insurance is quoted, not assumed

Premiums have risen dramatically in several markets. We obtain a real quote during diligence and underwrite that number, an assumption here has sunk otherwise sound deals.

We can say no after we go hard

Losing an earnest money deposit is painful. Closing a deal that diligence has disqualified is far more expensive, and it is your money, not ours, that pays for it.

None of this eliminates risk. Real estate is leveraged, illiquid and cyclical, and a sufficiently severe market will damage well-underwritten deals along with badly underwritten ones. What discipline buys is a wider margin for error, not immunity.
