What taxes cost you, and what real estate does about it

The mechanisms explained in plain English, and a calculator that shows the number most people have never actually worked out.

The Mechanisms

Why real estate income and wage income
are not taxed alike

Four features of the tax code, none of them exotic, none of them aggressive.

01

Depreciation

The code treats a residential building as though it wears out over 27.5 years and lets the owner deduct a portion of its value each year. The building is usually doing no such thing. It is collecting rent and, often, appreciating.

Because depreciation is a deduction rather than an expense, no cash leaves the property to create it. That gap between the paper loss and the actual cash is the entire mechanism.

02

Cost segregation

An engineering study separates the parts of a building that wear out faster than the structure does. Appliances, flooring, cabinetry, landscaping and site work move onto 5, 7 and 15 year schedules instead of 27.5.

The total deduction over the life of the asset does not change. Its timing does, and timing is worth real money.

03

Bonus depreciation

Property on those shorter schedules can be eligible for an immediate deduction in year one rather than spread across its life. Combined with a cost segregation study, this is what concentrates a large deduction into the first year of a hold.

Bonus depreciation rates are set by legislation and have changed repeatedly. The calculator below lets you model different rates rather than assuming one.

04

What happens at the end

Profit on sale is generally taxed at long-term capital gains rates rather than ordinary income rates, subject to depreciation recapture on the amount previously deducted. Under current law, heirs may receive a stepped-up basis on an inherited interest.

Depreciation is therefore best understood as deferral plus a rate difference, rather than as forgiveness. Anyone describing it as free money is selling something.

The limitation nobody advertises

Read this before you use the calculator, because it changes the answer for most people.

Depreciation from a passive investment such as a limited partnership interest generally cannot be used to shelter your salary. Under the passive activity loss rules, a passive loss offsets passive income. The excess is not lost, but it is suspended and carried forward.

Which means, for most limited partners:

  • Depreciation first shelters the distributions from this and other passive investments
  • Anything beyond that is suspended and carries forward indefinitely
  • Suspended losses can be released against future passive income, or on disposal of the investment
  • They do not usually reduce the tax on your W-2 income in year one

There are exceptions. Real estate professional status and material participation both change the answer, as can certain short-term rental structures, and each has strict tests. If you believe one applies to you, that is a conversation with your CPA rather than a checkbox on a website.

Why we are telling you this

Every calculator we looked at in this category quietly ignores the rule and multiplies the deduction by your top tax bracket. That produces a bigger number and a worse decision. Ours models the restriction, and lets you switch it off only if you actually qualify.

What this does not do

Tax treatment is a reason to prefer one wrapper over another. It is never a reason to buy a bad building.

A deduction on a deal that loses money is still a loss. We would rather you evaluated the asset, the debt, the market and the operator first, and treated the tax outcome as what it is: a meaningful improvement to the return on an investment that already stands up without it.
