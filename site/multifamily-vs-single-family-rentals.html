How Multifamily Outperforms Single-Family Rentals

Single-family homes are a common entry point. For passive income, scale and risk management, apartments are a different asset.

David Snider / 9 July 2025 / 4 min read

Single-family homes have long been the entry point for real estate investors. When it comes to passive income, scale and risk, though, multifamily is a materially different proposition.

A direct comparison

FactorSingle-familyMultifamily
Units1 per propertyDozens to hundreds under one roof
TenantsOne source of rentMany sources of rent
A vacancy100% loss of incomeA rounding error
ValuationSet by nearby comparable salesSet by the income it produces
ManagementUsually youProfessional, funded in the budget
Per-door costHigherLower, through economies of scale

Why the structure favours passive investors

  • Diversified income. One vacancy does not stop your cash flow.
  • Professional management. Built into the operating budget rather than into your evenings.
  • Valuation you can influence. Value is tied to net operating income, not to what the neighbour's house sold for.
  • Tax treatment. Bonus depreciation and cost segregation apply at scale.
  • Redundancy. One roof, many income streams.

On the cash flow comparison

You will see side-by-side figures quoted in this category, often showing a single-family rental yielding a few hundred dollars a month against a larger figure for the same capital in a syndication.

Treat those numbers with care. They depend entirely on the specific property, the market, the debt and the year. The structural argument above holds regardless of the figures. The figures themselves do not travel between deals.

David SniderManaging Partner, Chateau Capital

I started with single-family rentals. The moment I moved into multifamily, the difference was obvious.

More income, fewer headaches, and better long-term results. For a passive investor it is not a close call.
