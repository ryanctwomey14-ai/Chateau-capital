Why Does the IRS Offer Bonus Depreciation at All?

It looks generous. It is actually policy, aimed at a housing shortage and paid for with private capital rather than public spending.

David Snider / 24 August 2025 / 4 min read

Investors new to real estate often ask why such a substantial deduction is available at all. The answer is that it is not a loophole. It is a deliberate incentive aimed at outcomes the government wants and would otherwise have to fund directly.

The policy reasoning

  • Stimulate economic growth. Investment in real estate drives construction, renovation and local employment.
  • Address the housing shortage. Accelerated depreciation encourages investors to develop and renovate multifamily housing, adding supply where it is needed.
  • Support long-term ownership. Depreciation offsets the cost of holding and maintaining property across a long horizon.
  • Encourage capital investment now. A large first-year deduction pulls spending forward rather than deferring it.

What society gets

  • More housing. Purchased and renovated communities add rental supply for a growing population.
  • Local employment. Projects create work for construction crews, property managers and maintenance staff, and those wages circulate locally.
  • Better conditions for residents. Tax savings frequently fund improvements: maintenance, security, amenities and energy efficiency.
  • Environmental gains. Renovations often include insulation, modern HVAC and water-saving fixtures.
  • Community programmes. Owners focused on retention often fund tutoring, after-school and community-building initiatives.

What the investor gets

  • Reduced taxable income in the year the asset is placed in service
  • Improved cash flow, since less is remitted in tax and more stays invested
  • Equity growth as the loan amortises and income grows
  • Diversification away from a portfolio dependent on public markets
  • Leverage, allowing a larger asset to be controlled than cash alone would permit
  • Exit optionality, including sale, refinance or a 1031 exchange

In summary

For society, the provision stimulates growth, creates jobs and improves housing. For the investor, it offers the most significant tax advantages available in the code alongside a stable, diversified holding. Those two things are meant to be connected.

Leverage magnifies losses as well as gains. Every advantage listed here carries a corresponding risk, set out in each offering's documents.
