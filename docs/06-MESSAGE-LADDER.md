# Message Ladder

**Decision: lead with what the firm does, and what that does for the investor.**

The home page opens on capability, not on pain. Line one of the hero is the firm's job,
line two is the reader's position, and the two-tone brand treatment carries the split:

> **We operate the apartments.**
> **You own a share of every one.**

That single sentence pair is the whole positioning. Everything below it either explains the
work or translates the work into an outcome.

---

## 1. The spine

```
  WHAT WE DO          Acquire, improve, operate, distribute and exit.
       |              The business in four verbs, plus what we refuse to do.
       v
  WHAT IT MEANS       Four changes to the investor's position, each a direct
  FOR YOU             consequence of the work above.
       |
       v
  WHAT WE WON'T BUY   The buy box. Falsifiable, therefore credible.
       |
       v
  HOW WE OPERATE      One opportunity in two hundred survives five stages.
       |
       v
  HOW WE'RE PAID      Full fee table and waterfall, published.
       |
       v
  PROOF               Track record, limited partners, team.
       |
       v
  FIT                 Who this is not for. Then the conversation.
```

**Why capability first.** A reader who cannot explain what the firm does to their spouse
will not invest, however attractive the outcome sounds. Leading with the business also
suits the brand: a fortress monogram and "Built to Endure" pair naturally with competence
and stewardship, and badly with a pain-agitation opening.

---

## 2. The four impacts

Each is written as a change to the reader's position, never as a feature of the asset class.
Together they absorb all four investor motivations without any of them becoming the pitch.

| # | Impact on the investor | Motivation it serves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **You become an owner without becoming an operator.** A share of physical buildings with a rent roll, and none of the work. | Time, landlord fatigue |
| 2 | **Income you do not have to manage.** Cash flow from hundreds of leases, distributed quarterly as targeted. | Income, stability |
| 3 | **Capital in something real.** A building and an address, rather than another position correlated to the equities already dominating the balance sheet. | Diversification, hard asset |
| 4 | **Value that can be built, not waited for.** Apartments are priced on income, so better operations create equity. | Returns, growth |
| + | **Income the tax code treats differently from your salary.** Depreciation flows through on a K-1. | Tax |

Impact 1 is given a double-width photographic tile so the eye enters at the strongest
point rather than scanning a uniform grid.

**Tax is present, not leading.** It sits as a supporting tile with a link to the fuller
explanation on `why-multifamily.html`, and it explicitly hands the reader to their own CPA.
It remains genuinely differentiating, since a REIT's depreciation is trapped at entity level
and never reaches the investor, so it earns a place. It is simply not the opening argument.

---

## 3. On "behind on retirement"

Of the four motivations supplied, this one carries the most emotion and is the only one the
site deliberately does **not** sell against.

"Better returns, faster" is a performance promise in everything but grammar, and it recruits
precisely the investor who cannot afford a five to seven year illiquid position with no
redemption right. That is a suitability problem before it is a marketing problem, and it
contradicts the anti-ICP list in `01-STRATEGY.md` section 1.3.

**How it is handled instead:** the qualification section carries the line *"Are trying to
make up a retirement shortfall quickly"* in the **not a fit** column. Turning that business
away in writing is more credible than any claim the site could make in the other direction.

## 4. Hooks and tie-downs

**A hook** opens a section with something the reader cannot dispute.
**A tie-down** closes it by naming the agreement just reached and handing over the next step.

Tie-downs are what keep a long page moving. Without them, a reader who agrees with section
three still has no reason to reach section four.

### Hooks

| Section | Hook |
|---|---|
| Hero | We operate the apartments. You own a share of every one. |
| What we do | We buy apartment communities. Then we run them properly. |
| Impact | What that means **for you** |
| Buy box | What we will, and will not, buy |
| Process | Roughly one opportunity in two hundred survives all five stages. |
| Comparison | Honestly, against the alternatives |
| Alignment | How we get paid, published rather than buried |
| Team | You are not investing in a strategy. You are investing in people. |
| Qualification | This is not for everyone, deliberately |
| Close | Let's find out whether this fits your portfolio. |

### Tie-downs (16 sitewide)

| After | Tie-down | Leads to |
|---|---|---|
| What we do | "All of which is only worth something if it changes your position." | Impact |
| Impact | "None of that survives a badly chosen building, which is why what we decline matters more than what we buy." | Buy box |
| Buy box | "Every number on this page follows from that list, which is why it is worth reading closely." | Strategy |
| Process | "The sponsors who failed in 2023 skipped stage three. We would rather show you ours than describe it." | Strategy |
| Comparison | "Which makes the next question the only one that really matters: how does this operator behave?" | Alignment |
| Alignment | "Compare that table against any sponsor you are also considering. That comparison is the point of publishing it." | Waterfall |
| Team | "That is not a figure of speech. Look us up, then come and ask us the hard questions." | **Book a call** |
| Qualification | "If you read the right-hand column and nothing there disqualified you, the next step is a conversation." | **Book a call** |
| FAQ | "If your question is not here, it is probably the interesting one." | Full FAQ |
| Interior pages | 7 more across Why Multifamily, Strategy, Portfolio, Team, FAQ | Next page / call |

### The rule that keeps them from sounding like sales patter

A tie-down for a six-figure decision must never ask for agreement it has not earned.
"Sounds good, right?" would destroy the institutional register in one line.

**Three safe forms only:**
1. Name the reader's likely internal state
2. Anticipate their next objection and volunteer it
3. Invite comparison against a competitor

No closed questions. No exclamation marks. Every one is something a confident, unhurried
professional would say out loud.

## 6. Compliance guardrails

Counsel must review, but the copy already holds to these:

- Distributions are always "targeted", never promised. No figure is guaranteed.
- No quantified tax saving anywhere. The tax tile describes the mechanism and defers to the
  reader's CPA.
- Every target and projection is labelled as such, with the offering documents controlling.
- The footer disclosure appears on all 12 pages, and the footer CTA repeats the accredited
  investor limitation. `invest.html` gates on it. *(The duplicate line in the home page
  credibility strip was removed at the client's direction; the requirement is still met in
  four other places.)*
- Testimonials require SEC Marketing Rule review and disclosure of any compensation.
